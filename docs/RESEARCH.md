## History (for in-tour text)

- Founded 1738 by immigrants from three counties of Fujian (Quanzhou settlers per [S10]) as a branch of an ancient Longshan temple in their homeland; dedicated to Guanyin. Construction 乾隆三年 (1738) to 乾隆五年 (1740). [S1][S4][S7]
- The rear hall was added around the end of the 18th century. [S1][S7]
- Rebuilt 1919 to 1924 under Quanzhou master builder 王益順 (Wang Yi-shun). Most of today's fabric dates from that rebuild (大正9年 / 1920). [S1][S4][S9][S10]
- The main hall was destroyed by an Allied air raid in 1945; the Guanyin statue survived. Date differs by source: [S1] says 8 June 1945; [S10] says May 1945. The tour text only says "1945". Main hall rebuilt in the 1950s (1953–1959 by 王世南 per [S10]). [S1][S7][S10]
- The mountain gate (山門) was built in 1960: four columns, three bays, double-eave hip-and-gable roof (四柱三間的歇山重簷式山門). [S4]
- National monument (國定古蹟) since 2018. [S9][S10]
- Address: No. 211, Guangzhou St., Wanhua. [S5]

## Site and orientation

- The temple faces south (寺廟座北朝南). [S4][S8] Guangzhou St. runs along the front (south). Xiyuan Rd. Sec. 1 (the "Buddhist supplies street") is on the west; Sanshui St. is to the east. [S4]
- Land: three lots, about 6,149 m² (≈1,860 坪). [S4]
- Main hall height about 13 m. [S4]
- Overall form: "三進四合院之宮殿式建築" (a three-hall, siheyuan-style palace layout): mountain gate, plaza (廟埕), front hall (三川殿), courtyard (中庭), main hall (正殿), rear courtyard (後庭), rear hall (後殿) and east/west wing ranges (護龍). [S4][S5] The main hall stands alone in the middle, enclosed by the front hall, wings and rear hall (the "回" plan). [S8: "a central hall surrounded by protective dragons"]
- Plaza water features: 靜心瀑布 (the "purify the heart" waterfall) at the front left and a fountain pool at the front right [S3]. In temple terms left/right are given from the deity facing out (south), so the temple's left = east = the visitor's right as you walk in. That puts the waterfall on the east (right as you enter), matching [S12] (waterfall on one side, koi pond on the other) and [S11]. MODEL CHOICE: waterfall + koi pond on the east side, fountain pool on the west side.

## Halls

- Front hall (前殿 / 三川殿): five doors in total: three in the centre plus one each in the left and right side halls (前殿中間為三門、左右廳各有一門，總共有五門). [S3] Octagonal caisson ceiling (八角藻井). [S9][S11]
- The pair of dragon columns in front of the central door is cast in bronze, said to be the only bronze dragon columns in Taiwan. [S1][S2][S7][S11]
- Exterior columns at the centre of the halls are dragon columns or flower-and-bird columns. [S3] Main hall: four dragon pillars [S8] ("four pairs" per [S11]; secondary). Stone drum bases (抱鼓石) at the doors. [S2]
- Main hall (正殿 / 中殿): Guanyin with Manjusri and Samantabhadra, flanked by the eighteen arhats. [S1][S7] Three bays plus surrounding corridor, five bays in all (大殿有三間、加上左右廊，共有五間). [S3] Double-eave hip-and-gable roof (重檐歇山, main and rear halls). [S3] Round spiral caisson ceiling inside. [S9][S11]
- Rear hall (後殿): eleven bays (後殿有十一間). [S3] Mazu, the gods of literature (文昌), Guan Yu (關聖帝君) and other deities; Yue Lao is at the rear left. [S1][S6][S7]
- East and west ranges: 東廂 (left corridor, left wing) and 西廂 (right corridor, right wing). [S3] Octagonal stone windows with bat carvings in the side corridors of the main courtyard. [S2]
- Bell tower and drum tower (鐘樓 / 鼓樓): hexagonal, double eaves, with a helmet roof (盔頂) whose outline makes a reversed "S" curve, a first in Taiwan at the time. Often called the sedan-chair roof (轎頂). [S1][S3][S9][S11] MODEL CHOICE: bell tower on the east (temple's left, the usual 左鐘右鼓 arrangement) and drum tower on the west, both at the front corners of the courtyard rising out of the wing ranges.
- Roofs: covered with glazed tiles and decorated with dragons, phoenixes and other figures in cut-and-paste porcelain/glass mosaic (剪黏) and koji pottery (交趾陶). [S5][S9][S11] The wall-and-roof joinery uses no metal nails or braces. [S5]

## Colours (MODEL CHOICE, guided by [S5][S11] and typical southern-Fujian temple practice)

- Glazed roof tiles: golden orange with green eave edges; ridges in multicolour 剪黏 (green, yellow, blue, red, white).
- Columns: red lacquer on stone bases; the two front bronze dragon columns are dark bronze, visibly different from the stone dragon columns elsewhere.
- Walls: grey granite/andesite dado with carved panels; stone paving in the plaza and courtyard.
- Red lanterns along the eaves.

## Known limits

- Bay counts for the front hall are inferred from the five doors [S3] and the eleven-bay rear hall [S3]; real column spacing and depths will differ.
